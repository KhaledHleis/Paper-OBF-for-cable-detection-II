\subsection{Application Conditions and Limitations}
We conclude with several remarks on the validity of the cable OBF.

First, in Equation~\ref{eq:anomaly}, the first basis function is scaled by \(\cos\psi\). Thus, the projection of the signal onto \(\Phi_1\) vanishes as \(\psi \rightarrow 90^\circ\), reducing the signal’s effective dimensionality and potentially degrading detection performance for that orientation.

\highlight{Second, the OBF are orthonormal in continuous, unbounded time, but in practice they are sampled and truncated for use as templates. In our experiments, this had no noticeable impact because we used a sufficiently long window and a sampling rate that kept orthonormality errors negligible.}
