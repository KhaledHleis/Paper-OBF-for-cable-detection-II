Taking a closer look at the equations in Table~\ref{tab:basis_comparison}, the cable's field along the trajectory is, up to scale, $s(\tau) \in \operatorname{span}\!\left\{\frac{1}{1+\tau^2},\, \frac{\tau}{1+\tau^2}\right\}$ — denominator power \textbf{1}. The MOBF of order $N$ lives in $\operatorname{span}\!\left\{\frac{\tau^n}{(1+\tau^2)^{N+1/2}}\right\}_{n=0}^{2N}$ — denominator power $N + \tfrac{1}{2}$. So the dipole ($N=1$) decays as $(1+\tau^2)^{-5/2}$, the quadrupole ($N=2$) as $(1+\tau^2)^{-7/2}$, and so on. The exponent only grows with order.

For the cable's slow $(1+\tau^2)^{-1}$ tail, one would need $N + \tfrac{3}{2} = 1$, i.e.\ $N = -\tfrac{1}{2}$ — not a valid multipole order. There is therefore \textbf{no integer multipole}, however low, whose decay matches the cable: every multipole decays faster, so every finite truncation misses energy in the tails.

Regarding cable OBF in isolation, it depends on two unknown parameters $(\frac{v}{d}, \theta)$ where we must assign a prior value for detection, anamysis of the basis functions for varying values of $d$ and $\theta$ (see figure \ref{fig:x}) indicates that their behavior exhibits relatively limited variation in the angular range from \(90^\circ\) to \(40^\circ\), whereas more pronounced changes occur between \(40^\circ\) and \(0^\circ\), a range that is less relevant for detection. This observation suggests a degree of robustness in the choice of the prior value for \(\theta\) before the detection stage. 

Regarding the parameter \(d\), its influence is consistent with that reported in Anderson’s formulation and in \cite{Otnes2007}. Specifically, a relatively large prior uncertainty in \(d\) is acceptable when the source–receiver distance is large. However, as the distance approaches \(\sim 3\,\text{m}\) (which coincides with our operational detection range), the choice of prior becomes increasingly critical, since beyond this threshold the basis functions exhibit pronounced distortion relative to the recorded waveform.
